Course curriculum

57 lessons: 36 live cash-game lessons and 21 tournament lessons. Cash and tournament progress are tracked separately. Viewing a lesson is not the same as passing its questions or mastering it in live play.

Live cash games

  1. Positions and the Order of Play · Transcript
    Record position, action order, community cards, and effective stack before making decisions.
  2. Read the Effective Stack First · Transcript
    Effective chips are calculated per opponent; buying-in depth may differ from hand depth.
  3. Ranges, Combinations and Blockers · Transcript
    First eliminate known cards, then weight by action frequency; numerator of win probability is the hands you can win.
  4. Opening the Pot by Position · Transcript
    Initial raise range should be considered with position, size, rake, and blind reaction.
  5. Limpers and Isolation Raises · Transcript
    Isolate raise to capture value and position advantage, but cannot guarantee only one opponent remains.
  6. Why Fold When You Have Position? · Transcript
    Position is an advantage; whether to continue depends on range, price, and number of players.
  7. 3betting: Purpose, Sizing and Responses · Transcript
    An opponent's wide open range does not imply a wide continuation range; the 3bet size must be adjusted based on position and effective stack.
  8. Understanding the Price of a Call · Transcript
    Call threshold = call amount ÷ total pot after the call
  9. Outs and Drawing Equity · Transcript
    The draw must be clean; do not directly apply river probability to a single-call bet.
  10. Implied and Reverse Implied Odds · Transcript
    Potential payment does not equal certain payment; deep stacks may amplify losses of second-best hands.
  11. Board Texture, Ranges and SPR · Transcript
    Put your hand back into both players' ranges and use SPR to assess future investment depth.
  12. Value, Bluffs and Bet Sizing · Transcript
    Value: check if worse hands continue; bluff: check if the target folds, sizing and range work together.
  13. How Multiway Pots Change Decisions · Transcript
    A larger pot increases competition; don't copy single-against-single bet sizing and bluff frequency.
  14. Updating Your Read on the Turn and River · Transcript
    Don't base decisions on prior investments; reevaluate range, price, and actions on every street.
  15. Observing Your Opponents · Transcript
    Record facts, opportunity count, and scenarios; opening looseness does not equal looseness in calling 3bet.
  16. Bankroll, Mindset and Review Discipline · Transcript
    Pre-define acceptable investment; review decision rationale, don't judge performance based on single win/loss.
  17. A Complete Cash-Game Hand · Transcript
    On each street, compare current range and price. Results are only for updating future observations.
  18. Turning Preflop Baselines into Usable Ranges · Transcript
    First match the range table conditions, then check the action; coverage percentage is not win probability.
  19. Facing an Open: Call, 3bet or Fold · Transcript
    Yellow is conditional continuation, not random calling; use conservative default when conditions are missing.
  20. Small Blind and Big Blind: Price and Position · Transcript
    Big blind has discount, small blind often has no position; neither can use discount nor hand names to replace range evaluation.
  21. 4bets and Squeeze Raises · Transcript
    Dead money is an opportunity, not a guarantee; large additional bets are only responsible for the remaining range.
  22. Single-Raised Pots: Common Flop Textures · Transcript
    Board categorization helps generate candidate actions, but does not replace position, range, or opponent response.
  23. Planning the Flop in a 3bet Pot · Transcript
    Low SPR does not mean betting into any pair; even in strong hand types, consider the bet range.
  24. Check-Raising and Facing Check-Raises · Transcript
    A check-raise requires range support; facing a small bet can explore more aggressive counterplay, but not mechanically by frequency.
  25. Delayed Cbets, Probe Bets and Donk Bets · Transcript
    Update your range with prior actions, but a check does not automatically mean no strong hand.
  26. Overbets, Polarization and Three-Street Sizing · Transcript
    The bet size, range structure, and future stack depth must be consistent; MDF is not a required call proportion.
  27. Blocker Selection for Thin Value and Bluff-Catching · Transcript
    Blocking may reduce bluffs or reduce value; use actual combinations, not 'stronger hand' to decide.
  28. Combo Draws, Dirty Outs and Redraws · Transcript
    Probability of hitting vs. final equity differs; the error of the 2-4 rule increases significantly with many additional cards.
  29. Comparing 100 BB and 200 BB Play · Transcript
    First compare actual remaining stack and nut potential, then decide on edge hands versus a pair.
  30. Rake, Straddles and the Real Price · Transcript
    Range tables depend on actual structure; rake and live blinds alter edge decisions.
  31. Side Pots, Short All-ins and the One-Chip Rule · Transcript
    Clearly state 'raise to' a total and confirm the action according to room rules.
  32. Table Selection, Seat Selection and Adjustments · Transcript
    Look for situations you can understand and exploit—don't just focus on pot size or someone losing money
  33. Variance, Sample Size and Bankroll Discipline · Transcript
    Records do not equal proof; use pre-defined, tolerable budget constraints.
  34. Complete Hand: Getting Value with a BB Set · Transcript
    Build the pot using opponents' payment ranges; river size depends on which hands they're still willing to pay for.
  35. Complete Hand: The Limits of Deep-Stack QQ · Transcript
    One call does not commit to future actions; on the river, compare current range to price only.
  36. Complete Hand: Multiway Top Pair Facing Short-Stack Shoves · Transcript
    First confirm if there are any further bets, then use direct win probability thresholds.

Live tournaments

  1. Rising Blinds Make Your Stack Shallower · Transcript
    Record chip amount along with BB and ante structure
  2. Antes, Stealing Blinds and the Price of Defending · Transcript
    The big blind ante increases the pot, keeping BB unchanged in conversion; both opening and defense must be re-evaluated based on new pot sizes.
  3. Preflop Planning at 25–40 BB · Transcript
    Consider opening, re-raising, and subsequent plans together; do not blindly apply cash game stack depth calling strategies.
  4. Decisions at 10–20 BB · Transcript
    Short stack has multiple strategies; position, ante, opponents, and reward conditions jointly determine the choice.
  5. Shoving Versus Calling a Shove · Transcript
    First distinguish between stack gains and bonus equity. Do not equate shove ranges with call a shove ranges.
  6. Postflop Planning with Medium and Short Stacks · Transcript
    Consider bet size, relative hand strength, and opponent range together; don't let low SPR automatically dictate action.
  7. ICM: Why Chips Are Not Prize Money · Transcript
    ICM is an approximate model of prize equity; equal chips do not guarantee equal prize returns.
  8. The Bubble, Stack Coverage and the Final Table · Transcript
    Large, medium, and short stacks must be considered in relation to who covers whom and bonus jumps.
  9. Regular, Satellite and Bounty Tournaments · Transcript
    Different tournament formats alter the reward function and cannot share an unadjusted ICM or continuation bet strategy.
  10. Preparing for Live Tournaments · Transcript
    Recalculate BB after each level-up; pre-determine participation and rest schedule, and fully document key hands.
  11. A Complete Tournament Hand · Transcript
    Win or loss does not replace decision evaluation; after each hand, recalculate stack and identify learning points.
  12. Practical Baselines at 8, 12 and 15 BB · Transcript
    Push tables have position and tournament boundaries and cannot be directly converted into call push tables
  13. Opening and Reraising at 20, 30 and 40 BB · Transcript
    The same open-raise range cannot be paired with identical call and pot conditions.
  14. The Short-Stacked BB: Antes and Unmatched Chips · Transcript
    Ante goes into pot but doesn't cover blind; excess chips beyond matchable amount are returned.
  15. ICM Comparisons on a Four-Player Bubble · Transcript
    Prize shares must be recalculated based on actual results, not using a fixed risk premium.
  16. From Full Ring to Short-Handed and Heads-Up · Transcript
    Build ranges based on actual player count and position, with one-on-one requiring separate review of blind rules and strategy.
  17. How Bounties Change the Price of Calling · Transcript
    First confirm eligibility for the bounty, then calculate under a clear value model.
  18. Satellites: Prioritizing the Chance of a Ticket · Transcript
    With the same ticket rewards, more chips do not necessarily provide equal additional value.
  19. Late Registration, Re-entry and Tournament Investment · Transcript
    Late registration and re-entry represent new investment decisions and cannot be automatically inferred from 'already lost'.
  20. Complete Bubble Hand: Calling a Shorter Stack’s Shove · Transcript
    ICM should compare the actual post-action stack results, not just apply a percentage to the pot.
  21. Complete Heads-Up Hand: Shoving and Calling Prices · Transcript
    In a single-player prize structure with ICM, equity and chip count are linearly related; range and price must still be correct.
