Learning through decision trees

Start with your hand, position, effective stack and prior action. Then branch on the board, player count and opponent action. The same starting hand does not imply the same decision in every situation.

9-player cash game, blinds 1/3, no antes or straddle. To see the betting action, this tree shows the pot size before rake; actual table rake increases the required equity. Any unlisted board, bet size, or opponent range is not part of the expanded branch.

The edition contains 20 teaching trees and 191 nodes, alongside a 169-hand preflop index. It is not a complete postflop strategy for all 169 hands. Related examples explicitly use their own hands and assumptions.

  • AA: Value Betting and Strong Resistance — overpair is not a guaranteed all-in on the turn
  • AKs: Hitting or Missing After a 3bet — Strong pre-flop doesn't mean continuous betting on all flops
  • AJo: Start with the Preflop Situation — Position advantage cannot override all range and sizing issues
  • QQ: One Pair at 200 BB — Depth changes how pressure is handled later
  • 88: Hitting a Set or Missing the Flop — Reduce continuation betting in multi-way pots without justification; extract value from strong hands promptly
  • JTs: A Draw Is Not a Commitment to Call Every Street — Flop willingness to call does not mean a blank turn should be called
  • A5s: Nut-Flush Draws and Dirty Outs — Draw, made hand, and board pairing all require separate evaluation
  • KQs: Top Pair on an Unfavorable Turn — Subsequent community cards will alter both hands' ranges
  • AQo: When an Isolation Raise Still Goes Multiway — Strong preflop hands still need to tighten in multi-way pots
  • 55: Overlimping and Exiting When You Miss — Cheap entry into the pot does not guarantee profit
  • KK: Ace-High Versus Low Boards in a 4bet Pot — Low SPR cannot mix high A hands with overpairs.
  • JJ: Defending a 3bet Out of Position — First action means you must first bear the betting pressure
  • TT: Overpair, Second Pair or a Set — Same TT hand changes role depending on the board
  • AKo: Top Pair or Ace-High in a 4bet Pot — Low SPR favors strong top pairs, but does not justify a decision for unpaired AK
  • AQs: Top Pair or a Combo Draw Against a Tight Early Opener — Within the same range, made hands and draws have different justifications for continuation
  • KJs: BB Versus SB, with Postflop Position — In blind confrontation, BB gains position after the flop
  • 99: Multiway Pots and Players Still Behind You — Beyond the call price, must retain risk of a reverse raise from behind
  • AJs: Top Pair and Draws in Deep Multiway Pots — Deep stacks increase potential returns and amplify reverse implied odds
  • QJs: BB Defense, Top Pair and Strong Draws — Big blind has good price, but usually out of position post-flop
  • 76s: Deep Multiway Draws and Unequal Straight Outs — With suited connectors, consider how close to the nuts your completed hand will be

The ten expanded trees include action histories and remaining stacks. Some leaves fold or end a line; others identify additional range evidence or an unexpanded branch. Do not treat missing branches as solved decisions.

TT includes a stated flat-calling variation; it does not replace TT's 3bet baseline in the original CO matchup chart. KJs versus an SB open is one example, not a full BB-versus-SB matrix.

Choose a line, explain the reason, then return to a branch and change one relevant condition. Practice with live hand histories rather than memorizing hand-name rules.
