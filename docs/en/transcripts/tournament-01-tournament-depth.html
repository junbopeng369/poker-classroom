Rising Blinds Make Your Stack Shallower

Chip count hasn't decreased, why is action space shrinking?

Takeaway: Record chip amount along with BB and ante structure

  • Standard tournament beginner example
  • Full ante payment as per problem setup
  • Temporarily not discussing ICM decision

1. You have 30,000 chips

Blinds are 500 / 1,000, 30,000 chips equal to 30 BB

In tournaments, not only look at total chips, but also how many big blinds they represent. Now you have 30 BB

30,000 ÷ 1,000 = 30 BB

2. After upgrade, only left with 15 BB

Blinds raised to 1,000 / 2,000; same 30,000 chips, just 15 BB

Even without losing a hand, blind increases make the stack shallow. Future betting and raise options change

3. The ante increases the pot size initially

Back to 500 / 1,000, with big blind ante 1,000: initial pot 2,500

The big blind ante is an extra ante from the big blind player. Now the pot starts with small blind 500, big blind 1,000, and ante 1,000, totaling 2,500

500 + 1,000 + 1,000 = 2,500

4. Does the ante change the big blind's unit?

Blind 500 / 1,000, additional ante 1,000. How much is 30,000 in BB?

Note that the big blind ante is added to the pot, but the standard big blind remains 1,000. Chip amount refers to the amount paid for this hand's blind and any prior antes before.

  1. 30 BB
  2. 15 BB
Answer and explanations

Suggested answer: 30 BB
Yes. The big blind remains 1,000, so starting stack is 30 BB; the big blind ante is noted separately.

Alternative: 15 BB
The big blind ante does not change the standard big blind to 2,000. Still calculate the big blind as 1,000, resulting in 30 BB.
