A Complete Cash-Game Hand

Connect opening, value betting, range updates, and review.

Takeaway: On each street, compare current range and price. Results are only for updating future observations.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Preflop

BTN A♠Q♠ raises to 10, BB calls to 10, SB folds; pot is 21.

9 player, 1/3, effective stack 300, ignoring rake. All other players have folded. Practice this specific line, not claiming it is the optimal bet size against all opponents.

2. Flop hits top pair, top kicker

Q♥ 8♦ 3♣, BB checks; you bet 7, BB calls; pot is 35.

Assume earlier observations support the opponent calling small bets with weaker Qx. AQ can get value from KQ and QJ, while still accounting for stronger hands such as sets and two pair. Both players have 283 left.

4. River pair — where does the value come from?

River 8♣, BB checks again. If making a small value bet, what kind of hands are the target?

AQ now makes two pair, queens and eights, with an ace kicker. BB can still have 8x or a full house. Whether to bet depends on weaker Qx paying versus the proportion of stronger hands; compare checking as well.

Correct. They may pay value, 8x and full houses are ahead of you.

These hands are already ahead of AQ on this board and are not value-paying targets.

5. One possible outcome

Assume you bet 30, BB calls with K♣Q♦; final pot size 139.

Your total commitment is 10+7+22+30=69, final pot returned 139, net gain 70. This result aligns with the current value assessment, but a single payment does not prove opponents will always call.
