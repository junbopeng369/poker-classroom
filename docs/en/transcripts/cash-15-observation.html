Observing Your Opponents

From a single showdown to initial inclination—avoid premature player labeling.

Takeaway: Record facts, opportunity count, and scenarios; opening looseness does not equal looseness in calling 3bet.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. First separate into three behaviors

Entry method, reaction to raises, specific hand at showdown.

Observe after folding. Frequent limp-in, frequent raise, frequent call 3bet indicate different tendencies—don't replace all judgments with a single 'loose' label.

2. A fact that can be reviewed

Effective 150 BB, CO raise to 12, call BTN 3bet to 45, go all-in ATo.

This means he at least once called ATo with 3bet in this position and price. It makes you consider weak A-x hands possible, but doesn't confirm the frequency of such calls alone.

3. Frequency must have a denominator

Observed four similar preflop actions facing 3bet: folded three times, called once.

3/4 is a sample ratio, not the known true fold rate of 75%. Mixing opportunities with different positions, bet sizes, and stack sizes may further mislead judgment.

4. What does one bluff indicate?

You observe him bluffing a missed draw on the river.

Given only this single fact, which conclusion is most conservative? Successful bluffs that make opponents fold often reveal little about their hands, and intentionally showing hands may be a deliberate choice.

5. Next time, collect only three specific records

First observe two frequently playing opponents, recording position, stack size, actions, bet size, and showdown details.

Three is a convenient practice volume, not a threshold for statistical reliability. Unknown elements remain unknown; subsequent records should determine whether they justify minor adjustments.
