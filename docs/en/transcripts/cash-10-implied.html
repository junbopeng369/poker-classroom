Implied and Reverse Implied Odds

Winning significantly requires specific conditions.

Takeaway: Potential payment does not equal certain payment; deep stacks may amplify losses of second-best hands.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. What can you win in the future?

Implied odds consider potential future returns beyond the current pot.

Small pairs, flush draws, etc., may warrant continuation due to future payoff. Evaluation should consider hit probability, opponent's range, position, and actual willingness to pay—don't assume it based solely on their stack size.

2. Small pairs rarely hit a set

Pair on the flop hits at least one card of the same rank, about 11.8%.

For example, 5♠ 5♦, two cards remain 5, probability is 1−C(48,3)/C(50,3) or about 11.8%, including a few straights. A model relying solely on hits must have sufficient future payoff to compensate for most misses.

Pair on the flop hits at least one card of the same rank, about 11.8%.

Full spoken explanation

Suppose you hold pocket fives, with two fives still unseen. The chance of flopping at least one more five is about eleven point eight percent, including the rare occasion when you make quads. You miss most flops, so you need enough future payment to make up for those misses.

3. Deep stacks can amplify losses

Top pair with a weak kicker, low flushes and lower sets can cost you extra chips when they are second-best.

Such future potential losses are a key source of reverse implied odds. AJo meets AQ, both hit an A, and may continue paying; position helps assess, but doesn't eliminate being dominated.

4. Can a 20x call guarantee profit?

You call 15, opponent has 300 left, holding a small pair.

Must assess whether opponent pays with weaker hands, whether they'll be re-raised, position, and rake. Fixed multiples can only serve as a rough filter—not proof of profitability.

5. Deep changes, re-evaluate

From 100 BB to 200 BB, it's not about widening every starting hand.

Deeper depth makes position and nut potential more important, but entry cost must still be reasonable. When reviewing, separately record 'maximum possible win' and 'how much opponents typically pay'.
