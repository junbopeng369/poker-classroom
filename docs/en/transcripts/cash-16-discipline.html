Bankroll, Mindset and Review Discipline

Separate technical progress from short-term win/loss records.

Takeaway: Pre-define acceptable investment; review decision rationale, don't judge performance based on single win/loss.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Investment should be decided before the hand starts

Independently set a reasonable poker budget and current investment cap

The appropriate amount depends on your finances, risk tolerance and demonstrated ability at the stakes. No universal number of buy-ins guarantees safety or profit. A current win or loss should not automatically trigger moving up or increasing your bets.

2. First, keep the accounting clear

Your total buy-in was 600, and you leave with 450: a result of −150, or −50 BB.

1/3 of the big blind is 3. Record total buy-in, chips left, duration, and cost structure. One loss does not prove poor play; one win does not prove good play.

Full spoken explanation

In a game with blinds of one and three, one hundred fifty is fifty big blinds. Record your total buy in, the chips you leave with, playing time, and how fees are charged. One losing session does not prove you played badly; one winning session does not prove you played well.

3. Identify signals of declining decision quality

Only wanting to break even, rushing to prove, or forgetting position and price—these are all signals to recheck your state.

You can take a break, shorten the session or stop at the limit you set beforehand. A break is meant to restore sound decision-making, not guarantee a future win. Do not change the original budget because of one big hand or one win or loss.

4. Which hands to review?

You will select three hands to analyze from this session.

Review should focus on uncertain decisions. Hide results initially, then compare available actions. Final showdown can help update future ranges, but cannot pretend you knew the cards at the time.

5. Improve one point at a time

For example: facing a large opener from the early position, don't just follow because of position.

Replace 'play better next time' with one specific action. Check whether you follow it next time, then use more hands to assess whether the decision is sound. The final lesson puts the whole process into one hand.
