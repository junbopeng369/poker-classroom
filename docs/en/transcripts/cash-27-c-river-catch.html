Blocker Selection for Thin Value and Bluff-Catching

The comparison is against the opponent's continuation range, not your absolute hand strength.

Takeaway: Blocking may reduce bluffs or reduce value; use actual combinations, not 'stronger hand' to decide.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Thin value assessment when called by worse hands

Assume river bet 30, with weight of worse hands called 24, and better hands called 12.

4. Construct a range solely for calculation purposes

K♠9♦6♣3♥2♠; value hands are AK、K9、99、66, bluffs are only QJ.

Assume all legal post-bluff combinations are equally likely, and you only win against bluffs. Holding K♥, the value portion has twenty combinations. A real opponent cannot simply assume this constructed range is valid without evidence.

8. In practice, verify the source of bluffs first

Which draws were missed? Would they actually bet? What did your hand block?

After counting combinations, apply action frequency weights. When opponents rarely use these hands for bluffs, it is incorrect to call large bets simply because these hands exist theoretically.
