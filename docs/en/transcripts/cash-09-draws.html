Outs and Drawing Equity

First distinguish between seeing one card and guaranteeing seeing two cards, then calculate the return.

Takeaway: The draw must be clean; do not directly apply river probability to a single-call bet.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Nine flush draws

You hold A♥Q♥, flop is J♥7♥2♣; nine hearts remain unseen.

Thirteen hearts minus two hole cards and two community cards leaves nine. Start with the nine as clean draws for basic calculation; actual hand play must also consider pair formation on the board and opponents' re-draws.

2. Next card, both pairs visible

Flop to turn flush about 19.1%; guaranteed to see river about 35.0%.

The next card is 9/47. The probability of at least one of the two cards being a flush is 1−(38/47)×(37/46), approximately 35.0%. These are the probabilities of a flush on the turn, not automatically equal to the probability of winning the pot.

Full spoken explanation

With one card to come, the chance of hitting the flush is nine divided by forty seven, about nineteen point one percent. If you are guaranteed to see both the turn and river, the chance of hitting at least once is about thirty five percent. This is the chance of receiving a flush out, not necessarily your chance of winning the pot.

3. The 2-4 rule is an approximation

One card approximates 2%, two cards approximates 4%.

Nine outs correspond to approximately 18% and 36%, useful for quick table estimation. If you check-raise on the flop and may still pay on the turn, you cannot treat 'guaranteed two cards' at 36% as the direct equity of your current call.

5. Convert outs into reliable estimates

Count the cards first, then determine whether you might still lose or whether you can see them for free.

The goal of the 'outs' lesson is not to continue when you have a flush draw. First assess the quality of outs and the number of visible cards, then compare the cost; if there are future bets, proceed to the next lesson on implied odds analysis.
