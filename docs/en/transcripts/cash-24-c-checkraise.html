Check-Raising and Facing Check-Raises

Separate analysis of value hands, semi-bluff, and opponent's price.

Takeaway: A check-raise requires range support; facing a small bet can explore more aggressive counterplay, but not mechanically by frequency.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Big blind holds a set

BTN opens to 10, BB calls; pot 21. Flop T♣ 7♦ 4♠, BB holds 7♠ 7♥.

Big blind checks, button bets seven. Assuming the button will continue with top pair and overpair, these hands can pay for a set, and a check-raise has clear value justification.

4. A half-bluff must have improvement potential

J♥T♥ on Q♥9♣2♥, with a flush draw and an open-ended straight draw

It can evaluate as a check-raise candidate: seeking both folds and improvement. Whether to use it depends on the opponent's bet and continuation range; not every draw should be raised

J♥T♥ on Q♥9♣2♥, with a flush draw and an open-ended straight draw

Full spoken explanation

You can consider this hand as a check raise candidate, seeking both folds and improvement when called. Whether to raise depends on the opponent's betting and continuing ranges. You should not raise every draw.

5. When facing a raise, reevaluate the range

Top pair against a player with frequent strong draws differs from one facing an extremely tight raiser

First calculate the price, then assess value and bluff composition. Out-of-position players aren't limited to just a set; they don't necessarily bluff excessively just because they can
