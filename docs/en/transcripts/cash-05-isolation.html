Limpers and Isolation Raises

When someone else calls 3, reconsider the number of players, bet size, and hand playability.

Takeaway: Isolate raise to capture value and position advantage, but cannot guarantee only one opponent remains.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Two players have already shoved in

UTG、HJ each call 3; you have A♠Q♦ on BTN.

1/3, effective 100 BB, no ante and straddle. Add small blind 1 and big blind 3, and when it's your turn, the pot is 10. There have been prior participants, so reevaluate the raise size.

UTG、HJ each call 3; you have A♠Q♦ on BTN.

Full spoken explanation

This example has blinds of one and three, an effective stack of one hundred big blinds, and no ante or straddle. Including the blinds, the pot is ten when the action reaches you. Players have already entered, so reconsider your raise size.

2. choose a justified isolation size

For example, raising to 18 is a starting point for analysis, not a fixed formula.

Assuming the limpers typically call with weak Ax and strong pairs, AQo has value. 18 is a candidate size; the number of players, stack depth, and their reaction to price may change the answer.

3. Both players call, and be prepared

If both limpers call 18, the blinds fold, the pot becomes 58.

Each limper must call another 15. The pot is therefore 1+3+18+18+18=58, and you have 282 left. An isolation raise does not guarantee a heads-up pot; you can still end up playing a top-pair type hand multiway.

We've observed that limpers often play weak A-x and big pairs at this size.

Choose the primary equity basis. A loose table doesn't mean any hand can raise, nor does it mean a larger bet size will necessarily drive everyone out.

Correct. Value and position form the basis, but still need to prepare for multi-way pots.

Alternative: Raising to 18 leaves only one player
Your opponent's reaction cannot be guaranteed.

Alternative: Since the pot has dead money, any two cards are worth raising
Must consider hand strength, opponent fold rates, and risk exposure.

5. There are boundaries to hands that can reasonably call

Suited connectors and small pocket pairs still require the right price, position and potential future payment.

Slightly behind calls can sometimes be reasonable, but 'cheap' cannot explain all calling decisions. If someone raises again, the original price and range have changed. The next lesson will further distinguish pot range from calling range.
