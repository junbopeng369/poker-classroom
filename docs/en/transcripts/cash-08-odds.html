Understanding the Price of a Call

Same 20% equity, why fold sometimes and call other times?

Takeaway: Call threshold = call amount ÷ total pot after the call

  • River: opponent all-in
  • No further betting
  • Ignore rake and ties

1. Initial pot contains 30

River, original pot 30. Opponent all-in 15, you must call 15

This is an example of a river with no further betting. Separate the original pot, opponent's new bet, and your required call.

2. After calling, total pot is:

30 + 15 + 15 = 60. You use 15 to fight for the final 60

If you call, total pot becomes 60. Your new commitment is 15. When calculating the threshold, include your call amount in the denominator.

Minimum required equity: 15 ÷ 60 = 25%

Your equity is assumed to be 20%; the call threshold is 25%.

4. Raise the bet to 5

30 + 5 + 5 = 40; the threshold drops to 5 ÷ 40 = 12.5%.

Now only change the price: opponent bets all-in 5, and you still have 20% equity. The call threshold drops to 12.5%, and this call is profitable.

Call average return: 0.2 × 35 − 0.8 × 5 = +3
