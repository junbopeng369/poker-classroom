Read the Effective Stack First

Having a lot of chips does not mean every hand is played deep.

Takeaway: Effective chips are calculated per opponent; buying-in depth may differ from hand depth.

  • 1 / 3
  • No ante, no extra blinds
  • Chips calculated before hand starts

1. Convert chips to big blinds first

You have 450 = 150 BB; opponent has 300 = 100 BB.

1/3 big blind is 3. Your 450 is 150 BB; opponent's 300 is 100 BB. Always describe chips in big blinds before comparing hands.

Your chips: 450 ÷ 3 = 150 BB

Full spoken explanation

The small blind is one and the big blind is three. Your four hundred and fifty chips are one hundred and fifty big blinds. Your opponent's three hundred chips are one hundred big blinds. Express stacks in big blinds first, so you can compare games at different stakes.

2. How much can each side match?

Effective chips take the smaller of two: 300, i.e. 100 BB.

In this hand, only you and this opponent are involved. Each can match at most 300. Analyze this matchup starting from 100 BB effective depth.

Starting effective chips: min(450, 300) = 300

Full spoken explanation

In this hand, only you and this opponent are involved. Each can match at most three hundred. Analyze this matchup starting from one hundred big blinds effective depth.

3. Excess chips do not participate in matching

You raise to 600, opponent remains at 300; effective depth remains 100 BB.

Increasing your chips does not automatically deepen the matchup. As long as opponent still only has 300, the effective depth remains 100 BB.

The extra 300 cannot be matched by this opponent.

Full spoken explanation

Increasing your chips does not automatically deepen the matchup. As long as opponent still only has three hundred, the effective depth remains one hundred big blinds.

4. Multi-player pot: calculate separately

You have 600; A has 150, B has 450. How deep do you play against each?

Now there are two opponents. Calculate your effective depth against each individually—do not rely on the shortest chip count across the table.

Big blind remains 3; all are starting chips.

Full spoken explanation

Now there are two opponents. Calculate your effective depth against each individually—do not rely on the shortest chip count across the table.

Make your decision

  1. 50 BB / 150 BB
  2. Both go to 50 BB
  3. Both go to 200 BB
Answer and explanations

Suggested answer: 50 BB / 150 BB
Correct. Against A: 150 ÷ 3 = 50 BB; against B: 450 ÷ 3 = 150 BB.

Alternative: Both go to 50 BB
A's short stack only limits against A's matching. You and B can still fight for more chips, possibly forming a side pot.

Alternative: Both go to 200 BB
200 BB is your own stack. Effective stack size also depends on how much the specific opponent can match.

5. Take to the next hand in practice

First read position, number of players, and effective stack, then decide how to play your hand.

Next time you face a difficult situation, record position, number of participants, and effective stack against relevant opponents. Then analyze the range and price.
