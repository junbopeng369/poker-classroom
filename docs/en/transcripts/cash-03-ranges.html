Ranges, Combinations and Blockers

Expand 'he might have AK' into a group of hands and their relative weights.

Takeaway: First eliminate known cards, then weight by action frequency; numerator of win probability is the hands you can win.

  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Range includes multiple possibilities

A single raise typically corresponds to a group of hands, not a single specific hand.

2. Flush, non-flush, pocket pair

Without known cards blocking: AKs 4, AKo 12, AK total 16, AA total 6.

Different rank cards have 4×4 combinations. Flush requires same suit, so only four. Pocket pair is choosing two out of four: C(4, 2) = 6, and order should not be repeated.

Without known cards blocking: AKs 4, AKo 12, AK total 16, AA total 6.

Full spoken explanation

Two different ranks can make up to four times four, or sixteen combinations. Suited hands require matching suits, so there are only four suited combinations. A pocket pair uses two of the four cards of that rank, giving six combinations. Do not count the two orders of the same hand twice.

3. Known cards remove combinations

Community cards A♣ 7♦ 2♠, you hold K♥ Q♥: opponent AK has 9 left.

Only three A's and three K's remain, so AK is 3×3=9. Flushes only have spades and hearts left. Blockers may reduce value hands, but also reduce bluffs—don't look at just one side.

Community cards A♣ 7♦ 2♠, you hold K♥ Q♥: opponent AK has 9 left.

Full spoken explanation

After removing the known cards, three aces and three kings remain. Ace king therefore has nine combinations left. Only the spade and diamond combinations remain suited. A blocker can remove value hands or bluffs, so consider both effects.

4. Candidate hands don't mean betting every time

12 value combinations bet all the time; 4 bluff candidates bet only half the time.

Assuming all candidate combinations reaching this node were equally likely, you only win bluffs. After seeing a bet, you must reweight based on bet frequency.

This is the percentage of the opponent's value bet, not the portion you win.

Suggested answer: 2/14 ≈ 14.3%
Correct, the bluff weight is the value bluff weight divided by the total bet weight.

Alternative: 4/16 = 25%
This ignores that only half of the four bluff candidates bet half the time.

5. Range can be adjusted

First determine which cards exist, then how often they take the current action.

In practice, you can first divide hands into strong value, weak value, drawing, and air hands—no need to list every combination. Keep unknown when there's no evidence for exact frequencies; later lessons will teach you how to refine them.
