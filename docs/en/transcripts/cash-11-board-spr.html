Board Texture, Ranges and SPR

Evaluate the same hand across different board textures and depths.

Takeaway: Put your hand back into both players' ranges and use SPR to assess future investment depth.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. First, see which hands are helped by the board

A-high dry board and connected mid-low board textures have different range compositions.

Preflop raisers often have more strong A-x and large pairs; callers may have more connected mid-low hands. But actual ranges depend on position and actions—don't assume all flops should be bet just because you raised.

2. Switch to a tighter connected board

9♥8♥7♣ connects many pairs, two pairs, straights, and draws.

AQ has no pair here; opponents may have T9, 98, JTs, or similar continuation hands. Compare the overall connectivity of both ranges—don't directly copy the strategy from A72.

Full spoken explanation

3. SPR is the remaining stack divided by the pot

Pot 60, both players have remaining effective stacks 240, SPR = 4

SPR describes how deep you can go post-flop. With the same pot, remaining 600 and SPR equals 10. It's a planning tool—not a single number that determines whether top pair must be played all-in or whether pot control is required.

4. Is a very low SPR always a full shove?

SPR = 2, you hold a medium pair, opponent makes a large raise on a bad board.

A small SPR limits action space, but opponent's range and relative hand strength still matter. Having a lot of chips in the pot doesn't mean you must commit all remaining chips.
