Value, Bluffs and Bet Sizing

Align the bet's return with its price.

Takeaway: Value: check if worse hands continue; bluff: check if the target folds, sizing and range work together.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Value betting requires a specific calling opponent

You have AJ, on a J-high river, ask which worse hands would call.

On J♣8♦4♠2♥2♣, your A♠J♦ is ahead of KJ, QJ and JT, but can be behind 8J and stronger hands, including full houses made from sets. Value betting requires considering the whole continuing range, not just noticing that you paired the highest board rank.

2. Bluffing requires a discardable target

When you don't have enough showdown equity, consider which stronger hands opponents might fold.

I didn't hit, so I bet — that's not enough. It needs board and action support, and an estimate of whether this bet can induce sufficient folds. A pre-flop check may still yield half-bluff value from potential re-entries.

3. Pure bluff break-even fold rate

River pot 60, bet 30; if continued, must lose, need to fold more than 33.3%

Simplified conditions assume no future payoff; if continued, the entire bet is lost. The threshold is bet amount ÷ (original pot + bet amount). A bet of 60 requires more than 50% folds, differing from the call win probability formula.

River pot 60, bet 30; if continued, must lose, need to fold more than 33.3%

Full spoken explanation

First assume that whenever the opponent continues, you lose and have no future payoff. The pot is sixty. Betting thirty needs the opponent to fold about one third of the time to break even. If you instead bet sixty, you need folds about half the time. This formula is different from the equity threshold for calling.

4. What does 'bet bigger' mean?

You're considering increasing your river bet from 30 to 60.

Compare how a larger bet affects which weaker hands continue and which stronger hands fold, and assess your own risk exposure. Simply trying to intimidate or hide discomfort is not sufficient.
