Side Pots, Short All-ins and the One-Chip Rule

Understand with specific numbers which chip amounts can win and when you can still raise.

Takeaway: Clearly state 'raise to' a total and confirm the action according to room rules.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

4. Short all-in does not form a complete raise

1/3, A raises to 12, the complete raise increment is 9; B all-in to 20, only adds 8.

According to the infinite poker TDA rule used here, if no one completes a raise after, any player who has already acted can only call or fold. Players who have not yet acted may still raise, at a minimum to twenty-nine. Cash tables must follow room rules.

7. Total short all-in and single chip

If another short all-in is raised to 21, the cumulative increase faced by A reaches 9; the right may be re-opened.

Evaluate each player separately. In this example, after reaching full increment, A's raise to the total must be at least thirty. Another common rule: facing a bet of fifteen, if a player does not verbally declare a raise and loses one twenty-five chip, it is typically treated as a call.

8. clarify the action

Before betting, clearly define 'call' or 'raise to' a specific amount, without relying on guessing opponents' intentions.

The rules illustrated here are based on general principles from public TDA guidelines and do not supersede live dealer rulings. If an action is unclear, please ask the dealer for clarification before making your decision.
