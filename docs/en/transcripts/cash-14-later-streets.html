Updating Your Read on the Turn and River

Every new card and action can alter the original plan.

Takeaway: Don't base decisions on prior investments; reevaluate range, price, and actions on every street.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Leading on the flop can change

AQ has top pair on A♣ 9♦ 4♠ with two cards to come.

First identify which opponents are weaker: A-x hands that will pay, and which hands with draws or strength will continue. Plan for the turn, but keep flexibility to adjust based on new cards and actions.

2. Turn creates new connections

Turn 8♦: more straights and spade draws appear.

The range that called on the flop has different strength on the turn. Decide to bet, adjust the size, or check based on current range, not just because 'the previous street had a bet'.

3. River completes some draws

River 7♦: spade flush completed, connected cards may also strengthen.

AQ may still lead weak A-x, but the original target hand's willingness to pay and strong hand proportion have changed. Compare thin value bets to checking, and reevaluate range and price if faced with a bet.

4. Have already committed a lot—must I see through to the end?

Opponent makes a large raise on the river. Do you think 'we've already bet so much'?

The amount currently in the pot is the sum of all prior bets. The current question is whether you can get sufficient return for an additional bet. Past bets affect pot size, but don't individually require you to continue.

5. Post-flop review questions

For each street, write: what changes in range?

If the answer is always 'I have top pair', the information is still insufficient. Add opponent's continuation range, new completed draws, current stakes, and remaining stack. The next module connects these assumptions to actual observations.
