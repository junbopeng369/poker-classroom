Opening the Pot by Position

When all prior hands fold, establish a conditional entry baseline.

Takeaway: Initial raise range should be considered with position, size, rake, and blind reaction.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. First confirm all prior hands folded

Initial raise RFI, and facing raises or limps are different situations.

Outside the SB, start with a raise-or-fold baseline for unopened pots. Small-blind strategy has additional nuances. Do not use an unopened-pot range as your calling range against a tight early-position open.

2. Later positions typically have more playable hands

Same A9o: 9 players at table UTG typically fold; BTN usually raise when no one enters the pot.

Players behind who haven't acted have fewer options, and are more likely to have position post-flop. Specific edge cards depend on stack size and table conditions; this is a general principle, not a complete universal range chart.

3. Set the bet size to a executable starting point.

1/3、BTN、no one in pot: start by raising to 10 for about 3.3 BB.

Raising to 10 means the total bet in this round is 10. Maintain a consistent bet size for the same position and situation first, then adjust based on blind reactions. 200 BB does not mean the initial raise is automatically doubled.

4. Greater bet size, is it always better?

Against a blind, 10 will be called with weak hands; against 20 will primarily continue with strong hands.

Your goal is to increase profitability. Observe how the larger bet size changes opponents' and hand ranges' continuation patterns—don't just aim to drive everyone out.

5. First practical practice

Only record three decisions when no one calls: position, hand, sizing

Do not aim to memorize an entire complex table. First confirm you distinguish between initial raise and call, then observe how the blinds react to your sizing. The next lesson covers cases where someone has already entered before you.
