Shoving Versus Calling a Shove

The same hand can have different outcomes depending on whether it's an active or passive all-in. The sources of gain differ.

Takeaway: First distinguish between stack gains and bonus equity. Do not equate shove ranges with call a shove ranges.

  • Live tournament
  • Chips and ante as per problem statement
  • Strategy must align with reward structure

1. Active shove, with direct pot win opportunity

Opponent folds completely; you don't need to bet to win the existing pot.

The expectation of an active shove also includes the potential for winning or losing after being called. Whether it's profitable depends on fold rates, calling ranges, bet size, and stage of the hand—don't rely solely on your own hand strength against a random range.

2. Call all-in, losing the fold benefit

Opponent has already gone all-in; a call cannot make him withdraw chips.

Hands suitable for an aggressive bet do not necessarily work against an opponent's all-in range. Tighter all-in ranges typically require stronger justification for a call.

3. First perform pot odds calculation

Original pot 12,000, opponent all-in 20,000, you need to call 20,000.

With no other players acting and no ties, the total pot is 52,000, and the break-even win rate is 20,000/52,000 ≈ 38.5%. This step has not yet included ICM and other bonus factors.
