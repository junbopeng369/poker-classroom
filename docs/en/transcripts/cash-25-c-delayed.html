Delayed Cbets, Probe Bets and Donk Bets

After the flop check, who gains new opportunities on the turn?

Takeaway: Update your range with prior actions, but a check does not automatically mean no strong hand.

  • Offline 1/3 · 8–9 player table
  • Example hand explicitly notes effective chips
  • Range example is not a general GTO range

1. Delay continuation bet

BTN pre-flop raise, BB call; flop both check, turn BTN bet

The button retains a check range with medium and strong hands. When a favorable new card appears on the turn, or when worse hands still pay, a delayed value bet may be considered; it is not necessary to bet on the turn due to missing the flop.

4. Probe bet takes position

BB calls the preflop raise, both players check on the flop, and BB bets on the turn.

This is typically called a probe bet. The big blind can use a hit value hand to attack, or study a bluff with potential improvement or blockers, and should not arbitrarily bet two times just because the opponent hasn't continued betting.

5. Leading bet has a specific structure

The caller on the previous street making a bet on the next street is not necessarily a mistake.

When new cards significantly alter the nut distribution, a lead bet may be reasonable. Start with clear value examples at the beginner level, without expanding into 'fearing opponents checking and betting randomly.'
