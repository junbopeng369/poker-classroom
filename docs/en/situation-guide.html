Situations, value and avoidable losses

Use the decision you face as the main learning task, and the hand as one condition. The aim is better expected value given the information available, not simply calling less or winning every pot.

Get full value

What hands worse than this would still call this size?

Strong hands slow-playing too much, bet size is too small, and folding when thin value is available.

Call or fold

Do the hands that the opponent actually bets with (value hands and bluffs) support this price?

A pair against too many hands, including hands that are bluffable but folded too often.

Draws and outs

How many cards are visible? Is the draw clean? How much can you take back after hitting?

Repeats draw cards, treats two streets as one street, ignores reverse implied odds.

Bet or give up

Which better hands would fold? What improvements are possible after being called?

Bluffing against players who don't fold, or making random bets with hands that have showdown value.

Position and stack planning

Who hasn't acted yet? What actions are possible with current pot and remaining stack?

Missed a later 3-bet, multiple players still betting aggressively, strong hands only realizing at river that the pot is too small.

Combine situation filters

  • Street: Preflop; Flop; Turn; River.
  • Pot type: Still pre-flop; Bluff into the pot; Single raise to the pot; 3-bet pot; 4-bet pot.
  • Players still in the hand: Heads-up; Three or more players; Uncertain if players behind are in the pot.
  • Relative position: In position postflop; Out of position postflop; In the middle of other players; Position after flop undecided.
  • Starting effective stack: 100 BB; 200 BB.
  • Current hand strength: Top pair; Overpair; Medium-strength pair; Two pair / set; Straight / flush; Direct draw; No draw and no direct draw; Still pre-flop.
  • Board texture: A or K high card; Dry board; Straight interactions; Two cards of one suit; Paired board; Three or more cards of one suit; Monotone flop; No community cards yet.
  • Opponent assumptions: Weaker hands are willing to pay; Large bets favor value; Wide short stack range; Need further confirmation.

Tags describe the selected node, not the whole tree. A multiway pot may later become heads-up. BB is in position against SB and out of position against BTN. Starting depth is not the current SPR. Also consider the actual bet size, action sequence, rake and possible action behind you.

Value is not just call frequency

In a simplified river model, you hold the nuts and ignore raises and rake. If a 30 bet is called 80% of the time, the added payment averages 24. If an 80 bet is called 40%, it averages 32. The lower call frequency can still earn more. These are assumed frequencies, not live-player data; non-nut hands also need to account for stronger calls and raises.
